# Leave No Trace: Canyon Country and Moab Region

## Why LNT Matters More in the Desert
Desert ecosystems are extraordinarily fragile and slow to recover. Biological soil crust — the dark, bumpy, sometimes black or gray ground between rocks — is a living community of cyanobacteria, lichens, and mosses that took decades to form. A single footstep can destroy what took 50–250 years to grow. This crust is critical: it stabilizes soil against wind erosion, fixes nitrogen, and retains moisture.

**The most important LNT rule in canyon country: walk on rock, sand, or established trail. Never step on biological soil crust.**

## The 7 LNT Principles Applied to Moab

**1. Plan Ahead and Prepare**
Check current fire restrictions (common in summer), flash flood forecasts, and trail conditions before departing. Permits are required for some backcountry areas in Canyonlands (The Maze, Salt Creek Canyon). Download offline maps — cell service is unreliable throughout the canyon country around Moab.

**2. Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces**
Stick to established trails and designated campsites. In Arches, stay on trail, rock, or sandy washes. In Canyonlands backcountry, camp on slickrock or sandy sites at least 200 feet from water sources, archaeological sites, and other campers. The Needles and Island in the Sky have designated backcountry sites — camp only there.

**3. Dispose of Waste Properly**
Human waste: In canyon country, catholes (6–8 inches deep, 200 feet from water, trails, and camp) are the minimum. In many high-use desert areas and slot canyons, pack-out systems (WAG bags, Restop) are required or strongly recommended — solid waste doesn't decompose in dry desert conditions. Pack out all toilet paper. Pack out all food waste — nothing decomposes quickly in the desert.

**4. Leave What You Find**
Arches and Canyonlands contain irreplaceable archaeological resources: petroglyphs, pictographs, granaries, and middens. It is illegal to touch, mark, or collect any archaeological artifact. Oils from skin degrade rock art. Look with your eyes, not your hands.

**5. Minimize Campfire Impacts**
Campfires are prohibited in most of Canyonlands backcountry and discouraged throughout. Use a camp stove. Where fires are permitted, use a fire pan (required in some areas), keep fires small, burn only wood to ash, and carry out all ash. Fire danger is extreme in summer — check current restrictions every time.

**6. Respect Wildlife**
Maintain 150 feet from large mammals. Never feed wildlife — this causes habituation and often leads to the animal's destruction. Store food in hard-sided bear canisters or hang food in bear country. Ravens are extremely clever and will raid unattended packs; never leave food unattended.

**7. Be Considerate of Other Visitors**
Moab trails receive heavy visitation. Yield to uphill hikers. Keep voices low; sound carries far in canyon country. Leave drones at home — they are prohibited in all National Parks.
