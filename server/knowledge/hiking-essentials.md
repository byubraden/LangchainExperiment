# Hiking Essentials: Arches & Canyonlands

## Overview
Arches and Canyonlands National Parks near Moab, Utah offer some of the most spectacular hiking in the American Southwest — and some of the most unforgiving conditions. Park rangers respond to hundreds of search and rescue incidents annually, primarily due to heat exhaustion, dehydration, and getting lost.

## Arches National Park Trails
Most trails are easy to moderate and use cairn markers across rock surfaces. Highlights:
- **Delicate Arch** (3 mi round-trip, strenuous) — the iconic hike; significant sun exposure with no shade
- **Devil's Garden Loop** (7.8 mi, strenuous) — fins, fins fins, and 8 arches including Landscape Arch
- **Landscape Arch** (1.6 mi, easy) — world's longest natural arch; continuing past this point becomes strenuous

Rules: Do not walk on biological soil crusts (dark, bumpy ground between rocks). Do not enter or stand in ephemeral pools (sandstone basins). Climbing on or rappelling off any arch is strictly prohibited.

## Canyonlands National Park Trails

**Island in the Sky:**
- Easy: Mesa Arch (0.6 mi, classic sunrise), Grand View Point (1.8 mi)
- Moderate: Upheaval Dome (0.6 mi), Aztec Butte (1.4 mi), Neck Spring (5.6 mi)
- Strenuous: Gooseberry Canyon (4.6 mi, steepest in park), Syncline Loop (8.1 mi — most park rescues happen here), Murphy Loop (10.8 mi)

**The Needles:**
- Easy/Moderate: Pothole Point (0.6 mi), Cave Spring (0.6 mi), Slickrock (2.4 mi)
- Strenuous: Druid Arch (10.8 mi, one of the most spectacular views in the park), Confluence Overlook (11 mi), Salt Creek Canyon (22.5 mi multi-day)

**The Maze:** Reserved for serious backcountry — trails are steep, unmarked, and require topo maps and high-clearance 4WD access.

## The Ten Essentials for Desert Hiking
1. Navigation — topo map + compass; cell service is spotty throughout both parks
2. Sun protection — SPF 50+, UV-rated sunglasses, wide-brim hat; UV is intense at 4,000–6,000 ft elevation
3. Insulation — temperature swings of 40°F between day and night are common
4. Illumination — headlamp; afternoon starts frequently end after dark
5. First-aid supplies — moleskin, ace bandage, electrolyte tablets
6. Fire — lighter and matches; desert conditions are extremely dry
7. Repair tools and knife
8. Nutrition — 1 extra day of food minimum
9. Hydration — carry 2–3 liters for front-country, 4+ liters for backcountry
10. Emergency shelter — space blanket at minimum

## Navigation
Download offline maps before you lose cell service. Always share your intended route and return time with someone not on the trip. Cairns mark routes across slickrock — do not build unauthorized cairns.
