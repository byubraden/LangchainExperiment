# Gear Guide: Desert and Slickrock Conditions

## Clothing for the Colorado Plateau
Desert conditions demand very different gear choices than alpine environments. Heat, UV, wind, and dramatic day-to-night temperature swings (often 40°F+) are the primary factors.

**Sun protection is the top priority:**
- Long-sleeve sun shirts (UPF 50+) are more effective than sunscreen alone and don't wash off with sweat
- Wide-brim hat (3"+ brim) to protect face, ears, and neck — critical for long days on exposed slickrock
- Lightweight gaiters prevent sand and debris from entering shoes on sandy trail approaches

**Layering for desert:**
- Base: Lightweight merino wool or synthetic moisture-wicking shirt. Merino is preferred — it manages odor over multi-day trips and feels comfortable even when damp from sweat.
- Mid: A lightweight puffy or fleece for evenings and early mornings. Desert nights drop rapidly after sunset.
- Shell: A wind/rain layer. Summer monsoons arrive fast with little warning; a packable hardshell (GORE-TEX or similar) weighs little and can prevent hypothermia from a soaking rainstorm at elevation.

## Footwear
- **Trail runners** are the preferred footwear for most Moab hiking — they drain quickly, dry fast, and the low profile helps with friction on slickrock
- **Sticky rubber soles** (Vibram Megagrip, La Sportiva FriXion) are critical for sandstone — look for this spec when buying desert hiking shoes
- **Approach shoes** are ideal for routes that mix hiking and scrambling (common in Canyonlands and on routes to climbing crags)
- Avoid smooth-soled boots — sandstone is treacherous when sandy or wet regardless of sole type

## Pack Weight
For day hiking in Moab:
- Water (4 liters = 8.8 lbs in summer) is the heaviest item — plan accordingly
- Keep total pack weight under 20 lbs for a day hike; lighter is better in heat
- A 20–30L daypack is appropriate; larger packs trap heat against your back

## Key Materials
- **GORE-TEX**: Waterproof/breathable shell fabric — useful for monsoon season (July–September)
- **Dyneema/DCF**: Ultralight shelter and pack material; useful for lightweight desert backpacking
- **Merino wool**: Ideal base and mid-layer material; handles temperature swings well and resists odor on multi-day trips

## Navigation Gear
- Physical topo map (Nat Geo Trails Illustrated maps for Arches #211 and Canyonlands #226 are the standard)
- Compass
- GPS device or phone with Gaia GPS downloaded offline
- Satellite communicator (Garmin inReach Mini recommended) for backcountry trips away from cell coverage

## Climbing-Specific Gear for Moab
For sandstone crack climbing at Indian Creek or Potash Road:
- Crack gloves (Black Diamond or Trango) to protect hands on repeated hand-jam pitches
- Large cam rack: 3–4 pieces each in .75", 1", 1.5", 2", 3" sizes (Black Diamond Camalots recommended)
- 70m dry-treated rope (9.5–10mm)
- Helmet — mandatory for both leader and belayer
