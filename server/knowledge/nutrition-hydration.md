# Nutrition and Hydration: Desert Conditions

## Water: The Critical Factor in Moab
Dehydration is the leading cause of search and rescue callouts in Arches and Canyonlands. In summer heat (100°F+), the body loses 1–1.5 liters of water per hour during moderate hiking. Most people severely underestimate their water needs.

**Minimum water requirements:**
- Front-country day hikes: 2–3 liters per person
- Backcountry day hikes (summer): 4+ liters per person
- Backpacking: 4–6 liters per day in summer, 2–3 liters per day in cooler seasons

Start hydrating the evening before a big day. Urine should be pale yellow — dark yellow or amber means you're already dehydrated.

**Water sources in canyon country** are scarce. Reliable sources: developed campground spigots, visitor center fill stations. Backcountry sources: seasonal springs, potholes (treat all backcountry water before drinking). Many trails in Arches and Canyonlands have zero water sources — carry everything you need from the trailhead.

## Electrolytes
Drinking large amounts of plain water without electrolyte replacement causes hyponatremia (dangerously low sodium). Signs: headache, nausea, confusion, muscle cramps — easily mistaken for dehydration. Treatment: electrolyte replacement, not more plain water.

In summer desert conditions, supplement with electrolytes every 1–2 hours: Nuun tablets, Liquid IV, Precision Hydration packets, or simply salty snacks (pretzels, crackers, salted nuts). Target 300–500mg sodium per hour during heavy exertion in heat.

## Calorie Needs
Desert hiking burns calories at an elevated rate due to heat stress and uneven terrain (slickrock requires constant micro-adjustments):
- Moderate day hiking: 400–500 extra calories per hour
- Technical scrambling or climbing: 600–800 cal/hour

**Best desert-ready foods:**
- Nuts and nut butter: 160–200 cal/oz, no refrigeration, heat stable
- Jerky and meat sticks: high protein, sodium replaces electrolytes
- Dates and dried fruit: fast carbs, concentrated calories
- Dark chocolate: holds up to heat better than milk chocolate; 150 cal/oz
- Tortillas over bread: don't crush, lower moisture content

Avoid foods that melt, spoil, or require cooking in extreme heat unless you have a good insulated container.

## Timing and Heat
Eat before you're hungry; drink before you're thirsty. In heat, appetite suppression is common even when caloric need is high. Schedule snack breaks every 60–90 minutes regardless of hunger. Eat larger meals during cooler morning and evening hours when digestion is more comfortable.

## Monsoon Season (July–September)
Summer monsoons bring dramatic afternoon thunderstorms. Humidity spikes during monsoon season, increasing sweat rate even when temperatures moderate slightly. Increase water and electrolyte intake during monsoon months. Be aware that heavy monsoon rain rapidly raises flash flood risk in canyon drainages.
